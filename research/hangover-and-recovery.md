# Hangover definition, outcomes and calibration

Checked 2026-11-27.

**Verster JC, Scholey A, van de Loo AJAE, Benson S, Stock AK. Updating the definition of the alcohol
hangover. _J Clin Med_ 2020;9(3):823. doi:10.3390/jcm9030823.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32197381/
* The 2016 Alcohol Hangover Research Group definition referred to symptoms after "a single episode of
  heavy drinking" starting when BAC approaches zero; the update notes hangovers are not limited to heavy
  drinking and revises the definition accordingly.
* Used for: framing the morning check-in as self-reported symptoms after a drinking episode, without
  thresholds on amount.

**Verster JC, Stephens R, Penning R, et al. The Alcohol Hangover Research Group consensus statement on
best practice in alcohol hangover research. _Curr Drug Abuse Rev_ 2010;3(2):116–126.**
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3827719/
* Methodological recommendations for hangover research. Used as background for the recovery study
  protocol (outcome measurement, confounders).

**Van Calster B, McLernon DJ, van Smeden M, Wynants L, Steyerberg EW. Calibration: the Achilles heel
of predictive analytics. _BMC Med_ 2019;17:230. doi:10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7.**
https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7
* Poorly calibrated risk estimates can mislead even when discrimination is good; calibration should be
  assessed at validation.
* Used for: requiring calibration error and reliability curves in every report and in the validation
  gate, not only AUROC.

**Frank E, Hall M. A simple approach to ordinal classification. In: _Machine Learning: ECML 2001_,
LNAI 2167, pp. 145–156. doi:10.1007/3-540-44795-4_13.**
* Ordinal targets via K−1 binary "greater than k" classifiers. Used for the ordinal severity-band model.

Outcome definitions used in research reports (e.g. "moderate or worse" = self-rated severity ≥ 5/10)
are analysis choices recorded in each report, not clinical cut-offs, and should be reviewed with
clinicians for any study.
